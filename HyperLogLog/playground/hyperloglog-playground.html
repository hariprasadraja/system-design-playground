HyperLogLog — the harmonic mean

m registers, each holding the largest rank seen. Estimate = α_m · m² / Σ 2^(−R_j). Harmonic mean — one huge register cannot dominate.

SECTION 1 · THE UPDATE RULE

One register per bucket, max rank

hash → bucket = top(log2(m)) bits → register[bucket] = max(register[bucket], rank)
Σ 2^(−register[j]) = Σ 2^(−R_j)
estimate = α_m · m² / Σ 2^(−R_j)

Small range: if the raw estimate ≤ 2.5·m and some registers are 0, use n̂ = m · ln(m / V) (V = number of empty registers). Large range: 64-bit hash → n̂ = −(2^64)·ln(1 − n̂_raw/2^64).

The general form: α_m = 0.7213 / (1 + 1.079/m). This is the HLL bias constant, analogous to φ for FM. Derived asymptotically, not fitted.

SECTION 3 · THE FORMULA, BY HAND

Harmonic mean in one line

estimate = α_m · m² / (2^(−R_0) + 2^(−R_1) + … + 2^(−R_{m−1}))

For the tiny hand-checked dataset (apple, banana, apple, cherry, date, banana → 4 distinct): the harmonic estimate gives 4.0. ✓

SECTION 4 · WHY THE HARMONIC MEAN WINS

One lucky register vs the harmonic mean

8 registers: seven at the typical value, one gets lucky.

registers = [3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 10]
HLL (harmonic):  α·m² / Σ2^(−R_j) → ≈ 46.4   vs   FM-style 2^10/φ ≈ 51.9
Push the lucky register to 25:
HLL (harmonic):  ≈ 46.5   (barely moves)
LogLog (geometric): ≈ 273.6  (explodes)

The harmonic mean keeps the contribution of a big register tiny (2^(−10) ≈ 0.001, 2^(−25) ≈ 3e-8). The geometric mean multiplies 2^(−R) terms, so a single large 2^R can dominate the average. HLL is the robust choice.
