One store · the first empty bitmap position

The first empty box
is the answer.

Each customer leaves a mark on a bitmap. We scan from the bottom up and stop at the first empty box. That box is R, and 2^R is our guess at how many customers visited.

SECTION 1 · THE UPDATE RULE

One bitmap, one mark per customer

Hash each customer. Count the zeros from the right until you reach a 1. That count is the customer's position. Put a mark in that position.

A returning customer repeats the same hash, so it marks the same position again. Nothing changes.

hash(customer)  →  count trailing zeros  →  bitmap[position] = 1

SECTION 3 · WHY THE FIRST EMPTY BOX?

It finds the most common missing pattern

Boxes fill by rarity. Position 0 is common, position 1 rarer, position 2 rarer still, and so on.

Position 0: about 1 in 2 customers marks it.
Position 1: about 1 in 4 customers marks it.
Position 2: about 1 in 8 customers marks it.
Position 3: about 1 in 16 customers marks it.

The first empty box is the first common pattern that is missing. Missing a pattern that should be common is strong evidence the customer count is small.

SECTION 5 · FROM R TO THE ESTIMATE

2^R, and why many bitmaps are averaged

One bitmap

Many bitmaps

Each observer gets a different hash, so different first-empty positions.

Observers:  2   3   4
Average:     3
Estimate:   2^3 / φ ≈ 10.34

The final paper method uses stable partitioning and a factor of m.

SECTION 6 · THEOREM 3.A · PP. 193–195

What is the “magic” constant φ?

Theorem 3.A is the paper's theorem that produces the constant φ ≈ 0.77351 — the number we divide by in 2^R / φ.

The plain version: the paper proved that the average rank R from one bitmap comes out to about log₂(n) − 0.3705. Hari's estimate 2^R is therefore systematically a little low. Dividing by φ ≈ 0.77351 stretches it back toward the right customer count.

φ = (e^γ / sqrt(2)) × (2/3)
    × product over j = 1, 2, … of
      [ ((4j+1)(4j+2)) / ((4j)(4j+3)) ]^((-1)^popcount(j))

γ ≈ 0.5772156649   (Euler's constant)
Even popcount: multiply. Odd popcount: divide.

The first few factors are:

j=1, binary 1:   divide by 30/28
j=2, binary 10:  divide by 90/88
j=3, binary 11:  multiply by 182/180

Evaluating progressively converges to φ ≈ 0.773516.

The bridge from probability to product

1. Inclusion–exclusion gives the exact distribution of R.
2. A smooth approximation gives the expected rank as a sum over scales n/2, n/4, n/8, …
3. A Mellin transform reorganises those scales.
4. The main term extracted is log₂n + (γ + N′(0))/ln2 − 1/2.
5. From that: ln φ = γ + N′(0) − ln2/2.
6. Grouping terms four at a time gives the product.
